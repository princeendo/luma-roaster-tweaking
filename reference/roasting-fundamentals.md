# Roasting fundamentals

General coffee-roasting knowledge for planning and diagnosing roasts. Everything here is **external guidance**: widely used home and specialty roasting practice, not a claim from the manual and not yet tested on this roaster. When a roast on this machine shows something different, the roast wins. Record it in [roaster-profile.md](roaster-profile.md).

Numbers below are typical ranges, not targets. Most come from drum roasters with bean probes. A hot-air roaster with no probe can only use time, sound, sight, smell and weight.

## What happens during a roast

| Phase | What you see and smell | What it does to the cup |
|---|---|---|
| Drying | Beans stay green, then pale. Grassy, hay smell. Steam. | Removes water. If rushed, the outside cooks before the inside, which leads to uneven roasts. |
| Browning (Maillard) | Beans turn yellow, tan, then light brown. Bread, toast, then caramel smells. Chaff lifts off. | Builds sweetness, body and most of the flavor compounds. |
| First crack | Loud pops like popcorn. Beans swell. Chaff spikes. | Water vapor and gas break the bean. The coffee becomes drinkable from here on. |
| Development | Time from first crack start to the end of heating. | Short: bright, acidic, can taste sour or grassy. Long: sweeter, rounder, less acidity, eventually flat or roasty. |
| Second crack | Quieter, faster crackle. Smoke rises. Oil may appear. | Roast flavor starts to cover origin flavor. Bitter, smoky, chocolate, then burnt. |

Typical bean temperatures in drum roasters: first crack near 196 °C, second crack near 224 °C. On this roaster, use the sound of the cracks, not the display.

## Useful numbers

| Measure | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weight loss: `cinnamon`–`light` | 11–13 % | |
| Weight loss: `city`–`city_plus` | 13–15 % | |
| Weight loss: `full_city`–`full_city_plus` | 15–17 % | |
| Weight loss: `vienna` and darker | 17–20 %+ | |
| Development ratio (DTR) | 15–25 % | Lower for bright light roasts, higher for sweeter or darker ones. |
| Development time after first crack | 1:00–3:00 | Small air roasters are often at the short end. |
| Total roast time, small air roaster | 7–12 min | Much faster than a drum roaster. |

Weight loss is the most reliable number Colin can measure on this roaster. A kitchen scale with 0.1 g resolution is enough.

## Rest before brewing

Fresh coffee releases carbon dioxide, which makes brewing uneven.

| Roast level | Rest before first brew | Best window |
|---|---|---|
| Light | 5–10 days | 1–4 weeks |
| Medium | 3–7 days | 1–3 weeks |
| Dark | 1–4 days | 1–2 weeks |

Store roasted coffee in an airtight container away from light and heat. A one-way valve bag works.

## How green coffee changes the plan

| Factor | Effect | Planning response |
|---|---|---|
| Washed process | Low chaff, even roasting, bright acidity. | P01 (manual). Usually tolerates a lighter roast. |
| Natural process | High chaff, roasts faster and less evenly, fruity, can scorch. | P02 (manual). Watch for an early first crack. Empty chaff promptly. |
| Honey process | Between washed and natural. Sticky, more chaff. | P02 (manual). |
| Wet-hulled (for example Sumatra) | Irregular beans, uneven color, earthy. | Often better medium-dark. Judge by smell and weight loss more than color. |
| Decaf | Already brown, so color is misleading. Roasts fast, first crack is faint. | Rely on time and weight loss. Plan a shorter roast than the same coffee undecaffeinated. |
| High altitude, dense, small hard beans | Need more energy. First crack later. | Expect a longer roast. |
| Low altitude, soft, large beans | Roast faster and scorch more easily. | Expect a shorter roast. |
| Old green coffee (over about a year since harvest) | Drier. Roasts faster, tastes flatter or papery. | Expect a shorter roast and less vivid flavor. |

## Hot-air roaster behavior

- **Batch size and movement.** The beans must circulate freely. Overloading slows circulation, which causes uneven roasts and scorching, and on this roaster the E2 error. Underloading can make beans fly and roast too fast. A load somewhat below maximum is a common starting point.
- **Chaff and airflow.** A full chaff bin or a dusty intake screen reduces airflow, which slows the roast and can raise heater temperature. Empty the bin after every roast. Clean the intake screen regularly.
- **Room temperature and voltage.** A cold room or a sagging supply voltage slows small electric roasters noticeably. Use the same outlet each time. Avoid sharing the circuit with a kettle or microwave during a roast.
- **Warm starts.** A second batch soon after the first usually reaches each stage sooner.
- **Ending the roast.** Cooling speed matters because beans keep roasting from their own heat. This roaster cools in the chamber, which is slower than tipping beans into a colander. Consider the first 30–60 seconds of cooling as a small amount of extra development.

## Diagnosing the cup

| Taste or look | Likely cause | Next change |
|---|---|---|
| Sour, grassy, peanut, thin; pale crease in the bean | Underdeveloped. Ended too soon after first crack, or before it. | Extend development by 20–40 s. |
| Sharp and sour but otherwise flavorful | Light roast, short development. | Small extension, 15–30 s. |
| Flat, bready, papery, dull | Roast too long overall, or stalled before first crack. | Shorten the roast, or reduce batch size to speed it up. |
| Bitter, ashy, smoky | Too dark. | End earlier, before or at the start of second crack. |
| Dark patches on flat faces (scorching) or dark tips (tipping) | Too much heat early, or poor circulation. | Reduce batch size, check airflow, clean screens. |
| Very uneven bean colors | Poor circulation, mixed bean sizes, or a natural-process lot. | Reduce batch, sort out broken beans. |
| Great on day 3, faded by day 14 | Normal for darker roasts. | Roast smaller amounts more often. |

Brew every roast of a bean with the same recipe, so the roast is the only variable.

## Safety reminders

- Roasting produces smoke, especially near second crack. Roast under a range hood or with good ventilation. Smoke can set off alarms.
- Chaff is flammable. Empty the bin after each roast.
- Stay with the roaster for the whole cycle (manual).
